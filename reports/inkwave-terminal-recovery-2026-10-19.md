## Confirmed failures and minimal ownership changes

PR #1679 preserves the old world on a single late publication failure. Independent injection also found that an exception while restoring the old Minimap/Environment could skip candidate cleanup and replace the primary error. The unchanged Environment dock reconstruction can dispose the previous dock and fail again when rebuilding it. The retained old Environment pointer is then dead; preserving pointer identity alone does not make it playable.

The native-world owner now attempts rollback steps independently and guarantees candidate cleanup in `finally`, retaining the original publication exception. A successful rollback keeps the previous world available for retry. If rollback itself fails, `game._nativeWorldRecoveryFatal = {error,recoveryErrors}` and `game.frozen = true` establish a terminal page state. The native adapter does not restart attract or fade into play in that state. The menu offers explicit user reload and shares the Renderer `_contextRecoveryOverlay` latch, avoiding duplicate dialogs in either order.

PR #1682 pauses and blocks rendering on real context loss, but public resume/Start could previously resume simulation while rendering remained blocked. The common clock gate tests `game.R?._contextLost || game._nativeWorldRecoveryFatal` at simulation/frame/loop and resume/start/attract/network/quit entry points. Async startup flow/current and world continuations observe the same terminal predicate. Ordinary pause and successful stage rollback retain their normal behavior. There is no same-page clear of the recovery latch.

**This is terminal containment, not successful Environment reconstruction.** In the real dock-method double-fault case the old dock pointer remains removed/disposed, and geometry/material/mesh disposal is still observed twice in the unchanged Environment. Reload is required. Candidate display/paint/ink/material cleanup is attempted independently once; a cleanup exception does not replace the original error or skip siblings.
