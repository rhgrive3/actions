# Splatoon 3 ranked modes: research baseline for INKWAVE

Checked 2026-10-11. Target reference is Splatoon 3 Ver. 11.3.0. This note records externally documented behavior for the four Anarchy rules and highlights unresolved values before treating any of them as verified 11.3.0 behavior. It is research only; no Nintendo-console test was performed.

## Source hierarchy and version boundary

Nintendo’s [Ver. 11.3.0 update history](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/59461/kw/Splatoon%203) says the update was released on 2026-08-19. Its multiplayer notes list a new replica weapon, balance changes to main and special weapons, special-point costs, and a control bug fix. They do not list changes to the ranked-mode rules. It is reasonable to carry earlier documented mode mechanics forward as a working hypothesis, but the patch notes do not prove each rule value stayed unchanged. The [official Nintendo guide](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/ichikara/av5ja/03_en.html) confirms the four Anarchy modes and their basic objectives; it does not publish most of the numeric rules below.

The strongest public structured parameter lead found is [Leanny/splat3](https://github.com/Leanny/splat3), an open parsed Splatoon 3 game-data repository. Its current [`versions.json`](https://github.com/Leanny/splat3/blob/main/versions.json) includes `1130` (11.3.0). The companion [parameter viewer source](https://github.com/Leanny/splat3/blob/main/parameters.html) exposes Main Weapons, Sub Weapons, and Special Weapons, and loads `GameParameterTable` JSON by weapon actor. It can help verify 11.3 weapon or special-weapon parameters, but it does not expose a ranked-rule parameter catalogue, mode-object state machine, stage-specific objective routes, or all map placement data. I did not find a public 11.3.0 raw rule-parameter dump for Zones, Tower Control, Rainmaker, or Clam Blitz.

[DesperC/Awesome-Splatoon3-Hacking](https://github.com/DesperC/Awesome-Splatoon3-Hacking) is marked abandoned and gives useful file-format orientation (`.bgyml`, `.gyml`, `.byml`, packed game data), but it is not a current parameter dataset and should not be cited for numeric rules. The [Splatoon3mix parameter index](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/%E6%A4%9C%E8%A8%BC/%E3%83%91%E3%83%A9%E3%83%A1%E3%83%BC%E3%82%BF%E6%83%85%E5%A0%B1) links to Leanny’s viewer and labels its parameter information as incomplete.

The main secondary references requested are the [Splatoon3mix mode pages](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/) and [Inkipedia mode pages](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime). Inkipedia combines details across Splatoon generations; only explicit Splatoon 3 statements are used below. The Tower Control page itself could not be opened by the web retriever, so its cross-check is from the indexed page text and Inkipedia’s accessible Overtime page. None of these wiki values should be presented as Nintendo-published or directly observed in Ver. 11.3.0.

## Common structure

Nintendo describes Zones, Tower Control, Rainmaker, and Clam Blitz as four Anarchy rules; the Splatoon3mix pages describe each with a five-minute regulation timer and a count beginning at 100 (or mode-specific equivalent). For the implementation, use one shared match lifecycle but keep each mode’s overtime and objective state separate. Tie handling and end-of-regulation behavior need a team-order/tiebreak state: Inkipedia’s [Overtime overview](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime) says a no-score tie in most ranked modes is awarded to Team Alpha, while Clam Blitz has its own three-minute no-score overtime and Power Clam tiebreak.

## Splat Zones (ガチエリア)

The [Splatoon3mix Zones page](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/%E3%82%AC%E3%83%81%E3%82%A8%E3%83%AA%E3%82%A2) says each stage has one or two zones. A team captures a zone at roughly 70% of its area inked; capture immediately repaints the whole zone in the capturing team’s ink. The opponent neutralizes a captured zone at roughly 50% repaint. On a two-zone stage, neutralizing either zone stops that team’s count, and both zones must be captured for count to move. Inkipedia [confirms 70%, the 36-frame count interval, and the all-zones requirement](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Splat_Zones#Description), but its current neutralization sentence explicitly says 50% for Splatoon 2 and does not clearly state the Splatoon 3 value. Treat the 50% figure as S3mix-backed, not independently cross-validated by that Inkipedia paragraph.

The count advances one point per 36 frames (0.6 seconds), so 100 uninterrupted points take 60 seconds. Inkipedia gives this rate in its general mode explanation; the S3mix page describes a count by elapsed control time.

A team’s penalty is paid before its main count resumes. Time-up winner selection uses the main count only, excluding remaining penalty. Penalty magnitude is approximately 75% of the preceding continuous scoring stretch, but the exact algorithm is unresolved:

- S3mix describes 75% of fractional count progress, rounded up, and gives an 80.9-progress example producing 61; the page explicitly says the details need verification.
- Inkipedia’s [penalty formula](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Splat_Zones#Penalty) uses `0.75 × (start - end)`, rounds to nearest integer, and adds one if `start = 100`. That produces different values for cases such as a 100-to-70 stretch. It also documents a tie-point exception.

Do not silently choose one formula and label it verified. A Ver. 11.3.0 measurement should record the count before capture, the frame capture begins, any neutralization timing, count at loss, existing penalty, and displayed penalty. Preserve fractional progress until the loss event if matching the S3mix interpretation.

S3mix says overtime starts when the losing team controls the zone(s) at time-up. Inkipedia’s [Zones overtime description](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime#Splat_Zones) also allows the losing team to have lost control less than ten seconds before time-up if the winner has not retaken it. The latter trigger is absent from S3mix’s short rule statement and needs an S3 test. Both sources describe reversal of the lead as a losing-team win, a winning-team recapture as immediate victory, and an unretaken neutral state ending after ten continuous seconds. Inkipedia specifies the two-zone nuance: one zone neutralized/held for ten seconds can end the match, while immediate “winner retakes control” requires both zones. The S3mix page gives a five-minute overtime cap; Inkipedia’s mode page agrees.

## Tower Control (ガチヤグラ)

The [Splatoon3mix Tower page](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/%E3%82%AC%E3%83%81%E3%83%A4%E3%82%B0%E3%83%A9) says the rail route, checkpoint locations, and counts are stage-specific. There are two or three checkpoints. The tower stops at a checkpoint; its checkpoint timer progresses only while ridden, and multiple teammates accelerate that checkpoint timer. If the tower moves away before the checkpoint clears, progress drains quickly rather than necessarily resetting in one instant. After a checkpoint is cleared, it is passed without stopping again.

A rider from one team drives the tower forward. Opposing riders contest and stop movement. Additional riders from the same team do not increase normal tower travel speed. When the tower is empty, it remains owned by the previous team for five seconds; after that it becomes neutral and returns toward center. The S3mix page estimates empty return at about 0.8× normal speed and a ridden return at about 1.2×. Those speeds are wiki estimates, not an 11.3 raw parameter lookup. Do not import Splatoon 1’s rider-count speed stacking: Inkipedia search text includes that older behavior, while S3mix describes no same-team speed stacking in Splatoon 3.

Tower overtime starts if the losing team owns the tower at time-up. The losing side wins by taking the lead. The winning side ends the match by taking control, or by the tower becoming neutral after five seconds without a rider. Inkipedia’s [Tower Control overtime section](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime#Tower_Control) agrees and clarifies that the winner must be the only team with a player on the tower to retake control. S3mix notes a precedence edge case when both teams are aboard at time-up: overtime continues if the losing team owned it first and the winner joins; the same simultaneous contest does not trigger overtime if the winning team owned it first and the loser joins. Encode ownership history, not just a “contested” boolean.

Do not use one generic checkpoint duration/count for every stage. Inkipedia stage pages expose per-stage route/checkpoint tables; for example, [Barnacle & Dime](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Barnacle_%26_Dime#Tower_Control) lists separate locations, timers, and progress amounts. The public sources reviewed do not prove every route table is current for 11.3.0.

## Rainmaker (ガチホコバトル)

The [Splatoon3mix Rainmaker page](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/%E3%82%AC%E3%83%81%E3%83%9B%E3%82%B3%E3%83%90%E3%83%88%E3%83%AB) says the shield is a tug-of-war accumulator: damage from the other team is erased before the attacking team begins accumulating its own; simultaneous hits cancel. The shield breaks when one team’s stored damage reaches 1000. **The page labels the durability and weapon damage multipliers “current as of Ver. 11.2.0,” not 11.3.0.** Version 11.3.0 changes weapon balance (including several main-weapon parameters), so recheck shield damage and shot behavior against Leanny’s 1130 files or a Ver. 11.3.0 measurement before using the table as current.

In Splatoon 3, a stage route has one or more checkpoints before the goal. The team must clear the required checkpoint(s) before count can progress beyond them. Routes and count positions are stage-specific; do not infer progress from world-space distance alone. When the Rainmaker is dropped, either team may take it. The holder has a 60-second timer, cannot use ordinary main/sub/special weapons or Super Jump, and can fire the Rainmaker shot. S3mix says retreating even one step from the pickup point or entering the carrier’s own prohibited zone runs the carry timer at 4×. Inkipedia’s general article describes this as 3×, so the 4× S3-specific value needs a current-game check; do not merge the two descriptions.

The S3mix shot table (not explicitly versioned 11.3) gives full charge as 60 frames, direct damage 60, near explosion 180, and far explosion 40, with ranges 4.4 / 5.3 / 5.8. Partial shots require at least 20 frames and deal 30–45. It also says shots use no ink and award no special points. Cite those as wiki-listed values until matched to an 11.3 game-data asset. The [Inkipedia Rainmaker article](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Rainmaker#Effects_on_holding_the_Rainmaker) cross-checks 60-second carry, shooting, and carrier restrictions, but some of its movement/timer language is generation-mixed.

Overtime trigger conditions documented by S3mix are: losing team holds the Rainmaker at regulation end; the winning team’s latest holder was splatted within 15 seconds of the end and nobody has picked it up; or the losing team cleared a checkpoint within 15 seconds of the end and nobody has picked it up. The [Inkipedia Overtime page](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime#Rainmaker) agrees on the 15-second regulation qualifier for the losing team’s checkpoint breakthrough, and adds the winning-team-holder splat trigger. There is a source disagreement on the no-pickup period: S3mix gives 15 seconds after a qualifying drop/checkpoint, while Inkipedia gives 10 seconds after overtime starts or the losing team breaks its checkpoint. Both say overtime ends if the winning team takes the Rainmaker, the losing carrier is splatted, or the losing team passes the winner’s count. The shield’s popped/intact status does not matter to whether the Rainmaker is considered available. A current 11.3 test is required to settle the post-trigger pickup window.

## Clam Blitz (ガチアサリ)

The [Splatoon3mix Clam page](https://wikiwiki.jp/splatoon3mix/%E3%82%AC%E3%83%81%E3%82%A2%E3%82%B5%E3%83%AA) says a player can hold seven clams; eight automatically form one Power Clam. Its stage object cap is 40 total clams across held and dropped objects, with a Power Clam counting as one for the cap. During play, clams spawn at stage-specific, mirrored locations in groups of three, normally every 12 seconds; when total clams are 18 or fewer, the interval is four seconds. The page also says groups spawn every four seconds during a 0–0 overtime, and a final group can be partial at the cap (two if total is 38, one if total is 39). Inkipedia’s [Splatoon 3-specific changes](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Clam_Blitz#History) cross-check groups of three and eight-per-Power-Clam. The article also says there are 30 initial clams, death drops are capped at three, and loose clams disappear after ten seconds, but it does not version-qualify all of those rows; elsewhere it mentions a 50-clam cap. Use the S3mix 40-cap rule as the Splatoon 3 source and measure initial placement per stage.

Death drops one clam when holding one, two when holding 2–4, and three when holding 5–7. Loose clams can be collected by either team and despawn after ten seconds. A Power Clam is visible while swimming and cannot be picked up by the opposing team. Opening an enemy basket awards 20 count, then ordinary clams add 3 count and 3 seconds, while a Power Clam adds 20 count and 10 seconds. Chance-time duration is capped at ten seconds; after closing, the defending basket sleeps for ten seconds. The page says a persistent counter Power Clam appears at the broken team’s basket after a chance time, unless an earlier one remains.

When the basket closes, the scoring team gets a penalty equal to half the points scored during that chance time, including points that reduced a pre-existing penalty. The S3mix table expresses this as +1.5 penalty per ordinary clam and +10 per Power Clam. Inkipedia [says half rounded down](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Clam_Blitz#Scoring). Those statements diverge whenever the effective score is odd; test an isolated +3 score and a score that first consumes an old penalty. Keep half-count precision internally until 11.3 measurements resolve it.

For scored games, S3mix says overtime begins when the losing team has an active chance time or a Power Clam somewhere on the stage, including held or unexpired loose Power Clams; overtime is 20 seconds unless a chance time starts, in which case it lasts until that chance time closes. Ends include the loser taking the lead, the winner scoring, the losing team losing all Power Clams before opening the winner’s basket, the 20-second window expiring without a chance time, or the losing team’s chance time closing after overtime has started. Inkipedia’s [Clam Blitz overtime section](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Clam_Blitz#Overtime_rules) lists equivalent trigger branches with barrier-state detail: winner basket open, or loser basket open when it was opened while they led. No-score games enter a three-minute overtime; a score wins immediately, otherwise the team that formed more Power Clams over the full eight minutes wins one count, with Team Alpha breaking a tie. The mode-specific Inkipedia page agrees on three minutes. The general [Overtime overview](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Overtime) has a conflicting five-minute statement, so do not use that general sentence for Clam Blitz.

## Open measurements before claiming Ver. 11.3 fidelity

1. Zones: capture/neutral thresholds at the exact area ratio, instant repaint behavior, two-zone controller state, count tick phase, penalty rounding for 100-start and non-100-start captures, tie-point ordering, recent-loss overtime trigger, and 10-second neutral grace.
2. Tower: current stage route/checkpoint tables, checkpoint duration and multi-rider acceleration, progress decay, capture ownership timeout, return-speed ratios, and both-team-on-tower overtime precedence.
3. Rainmaker: 11.3 shield durability and every relevant attack multiplier; charge timing/damage/range; exact 3× vs 4× retreat timer; map route/checkpoint count and count values; 10s vs 15s overtime pickup grace.
4. Clam Blitz: stage initial/spawn locations, 40-object cap and spawn cadence, death-drop count, basket timing/counter Power Clam behavior, half-penalty rounding, score-differential overtime branches, and three-minute no-score tiebreak.

Record every measurement with software version, stage/rule, team and score state, objective state, input/event timing, and whether the evidence came from Nintendo hardware, browser simulation, or isolated logic. Until then, the values above are documented targets with the stated source limits, not proof of 11.3.0 parity.
